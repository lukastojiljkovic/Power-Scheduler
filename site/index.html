What it does

Windows can shut down on a timer with shutdown /s /t 5400, if you remember the flags, do the math in seconds and trust a command you can't see counting down. Pwrschdlr does the same in a window you can see, and lets you change your mind.

Five actions

Shut down, restart, sleep, hibernate or sign out. Actions your PC can't do, such as hibernating when hibernation is off, are grayed out with the reason.

In a while, or at a time

Set how long to wait, with presets from 15 minutes to 4 hours, or pick a time of day. Daylight saving time is handled.

A countdown you can see

A ring empties as the time runs out, and the taskbar shows the time left. Cancel or add 15 minutes at any time.

Close it, keep the timer

The timer keeps running when Pwrschdlr is closed. Nothing runs in the background in the meantime.

A last chance

Before the end, Pwrschdlr opens again and counts down in a warning that stays on top, so you can save your work, cancel or postpone.

Never late

If your PC was off or asleep when the timer ran out, Pwrschdlr tells you and does nothing, instead of shutting down when you come back.

How it works

Pwrschdlr keeps the timer in Windows itself, so closing the app, or a crash, doesn't lose it.

  1. A scheduled task, not a background app

    Starting a timer adds a one-time task to Windows Task Scheduler that opens Pwrschdlr when the warning starts. The task runs as you, without administrator rights, and is removed when the timer ends or you cancel it.

  2. One timer

    The timer is stored in your part of the registry. Starting a new one replaces it, and changing the warning moves the task.

  3. Windows does the action

    Shut down, restart, sign out and hibernate use shutdown.exe. Sleep uses the Windows power API, and on Modern Standby PCs, which have no classic sleep, it turns off the display, which is how those PCs go to sleep.

  4. Late is never

    When the warning ends, Pwrschdlr acts only if the end has just passed. A timer that ran out while the PC was off, asleep or signed out is reported instead.

Tested, and honest about limits

How it's verified

  • 55 unit tests cover the timer's phases, postponing, countdowns and times across daylight saving time, the registry storage, the shutdown.exe arguments and a real round trip through Task Scheduler.
  • UI Automation on Windows 11 Pro 26H2 checks setting, cancelling and postponing a timer, the warning, the task opening a closed Pwrschdlr, a missed timer and both themes.
  • CI builds the installer, then installs and uninstalls it silently on every push, checking that nothing is left behind.

What it can't do

  • Run while you're signed out. If you sign out, or your PC is off or asleep when the timer runs out, nothing happens.
  • Overrule Windows. An app with unsaved work or a policy can hold up the action, unless you let Pwrschdlr close apps without asking.
  • Plan days ahead. Timers can be up to 23 hours 59 minutes away.

Questions

How do you say Pwrschdlr?

Power scheduler. It's the same name without the vowels.

Is Pwrschdlr free?

Yes. Pwrschdlr is free and open source under the MIT License. It has no ads, accounts or telemetry.

What happens if I close Pwrschdlr?

The timer keeps running. A scheduled task opens Pwrschdlr again 30 seconds, 1, 2 or 5 minutes before the end, and it counts down in a warning that stays on top, so you can still cancel or postpone.

What if my PC is asleep or off when the timer runs out?

Nothing happens. Pwrschdlr tells you about the missed timer the next time it opens, and never runs it late.

Does Pwrschdlr need administrator rights?

No. Setup installs it for your account only, and the timer's scheduled task runs as you, without administrator rights.

Does Pwrschdlr collect any data?

No. Pwrschdlr doesn't connect to the internet. Its settings and timer stay in your registry. See the privacy statement.

Why does Windows SmartScreen warn about the installer?

The installer isn't code-signed yet. Check that its SHA-256 hash matches the one in the release notes (Get-FileHash in PowerShell), then choose More info and Run anyway.

Get Pwrschdlr

One installer, no administrator approval, nothing else to install. Pwrschdlr starts from the Start menu and uninstalls from Settings, Apps, removing its settings and timer.
